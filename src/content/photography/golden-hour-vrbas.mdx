I set an alarm for 18:40. Not because I'm disciplined, but because I checked the weather in the morning and the forecast said partly cloudy — which is photographer language for *get out of bed*.

Charlie came with me. He always comes, which means I'm slower than I'd like and occasionally have to wait for him to finish investigating a smell that is apparently more important than the light disappearing.

## The shot I didn't plan

I had the 70-200mm on and was planning to shoot the Venezia  bridge from the north bank. Standard composition, reliable, slightly boring. But the light hit the surface at an angle I hadn't seen before — a long orange stripe down the centre of the river, perfectly still because there was no wind.

I switched to the 40mm f/2.8 and shot it wide open. The stripe. Nothing else. No bridge, no context. Just water and light and the faint outline of the far bank in the upper third.

I took eleven frames. One is good. This is how it usually goes.

## On returning to the same place

I've photographed the Vrbas probably few thousand times. It's  teaching me things about light that a textbook couldn't — not because I'm especially observant, but because repetition eventually forces you to notice what changed.

Today what changed was a thin layer of moisture in the air from overnight rain. Enough to diffuse the direct sun slightly. Enough to make the shadows softer than usual.

You only notice this if you've stood in the same spot, over and over, again, soaking up everything around you like a sponge. Like I do, every time when I'm on the river. Me & Charlie.
