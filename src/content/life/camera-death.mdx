It happened the way these things usually do, without warning, and at the worst possible moment.  
The spring is in full swing, and I was about to test my new ND and polarizer filters and already making plans and scouting shooting locations. What a fool. Gods are often laughing when humans make plans, and that was the case this time.

I was carrying my Canon EOS 5D Mark III on my shoulder, not doing anything dramatic with it, when I noticed something was off. Some weird sound was coming from it, and when I brought it up to check, the LCD was purple. Not a color cast, not a glitch I could shake off but pure, solid purple. Then a generic \`Err\`message with no code attached to it appeared on the top display. And then nothing. The camera went completely silent and dead, like someone had pulled the plug on it.

For a camera that has been my main shooting tool for years, that's not a small thing. I don’t have a spare, and without a DSLR, I feel like part of my body is gone.

### The Diagnosis

My first instinct was to go through the usual checklist: pulling the battery, using a different battery, removing the cards, and leaving it overnight. I went through all of it. The coin cell backup battery was also a suspect for a while, since those can cause weird boot failures when they finally die after years of use. But none of the standard resets made any difference, nor the brand new coin battery.

After some research it became clear this was more than a firmware hiccup or a drained backup cell. The power unit had failed. That small purple flash before shutdown was the camera's DIGIC processor trying to initialize with a board that could no longer properly supply it.

### The Fix

This is where my younger brother stepped in. Starman. He's a high-level engineer and repair technician, the kind of person who has, among other things, worked on NATO ISAF server installations in Afghanistan and Iraq. A Canon power board is not exactly a challenge for someone with that background, because he can repair almost anything. Like he did with this camera.

He found the replacement part on eBay, in Japan, ordered it, and once it arrived, he went to work. The job required a digital magnifier, a steady hand, and a good YouTube teardown video for reference. The 5D Mark III is not a simple camera to get into,  there are a lot of small screws in different sizes and ribbon cables involved and not a lot of room for error.

But he got it done.

### Back in Action

When the camera powered on again for the first time after the repair, that startup screen felt genuinely satisfying to see. Everything functional, settings intact, no errors. Ready to capture some photons and save moments in time. Yeah baby\!

It is a good reminder that a lot of gear that seems terminally dead is actually fixable, if you know what you are looking for and have someone with the right skills and patience to go in and do it. A replacement part on eBay and a few hours of focused work, and a professional camera body comes back to life instead of ending up in a drawer or a bin.

Also, check your power unit if your camera ever shows a purple display followed by a codeless error. That combination is a specific symptom, and now I know exactly what it means. Dead power board. Be well and healthy, whoever you are, and cherish your brothers and sisters, your family, as much as you can.

I hope you enjoyed the story.
