I have an Ibanez amp and bass guitar, and the bass is better than the amp. I've had it for idk how long. I practice irregularly, which is the honest word for what some people call casually.

## What I can actually play

Some walking bass lines, blues, meditations. Bass is a powerful thing. The intro to a few songs I like. A handful of scales, somewhat reliably. Anything in E, reasonably confidently. Anything requiring fast position shifts, I'm like a water, if I like it we will flow.

I can hold a groove. This is the thing I'm most pleased about. You can put a click track on at 80bpm and I will stay on it for several minutes without embarrassing myself. This took about a year to develop and it is, I've read, the actual foundation of playing bass rather than just owning one.

## Why I don't try to get better faster

I could hire a teacher. I know this. I've thought about it several times and not done it, and I've been trying to figure out why.

I think it's because the bass is the one thing in my life that has no output requirement. The camera produces photographs. The code produces tools that other things depend on. The writing produces posts. The bass produces nothing except a slightly annoyed Charlie when I play something that isn't in tune.

There's no deadline on learning it. There's no version of "done." I can pick it up for twenty minutes on a Tuesday evening and put it back down and that is a complete and valid interaction with it.

## The amp

I'm going to keep the amp. It has a certain character.
