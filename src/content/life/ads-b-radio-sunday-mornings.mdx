Sunday mornings, before 7am, are the best time to run ADS-B. Not because the data is different, the aircraft don't care what day it is, but because the house is quiet enough that you can hear the RTL-SDR V2 dongle's fan (it doesn't have one, which proves the point) and the only interruption is Charlie asking to go outside.

I've been tracking aircraft over Banja Luka for about two years now. The RTL-SDR V2 sits near the window with a small antenna, feeding data to a decoder on the MacPro, which displays it on a local map. Range varies, on a good day with the right atmospheric conditions, I've seen aircraft at 400km.

## What you're actually looking at

ADS-B is a system where aircraft broadcast their position, altitude, speed and identity several times per second. Any receiver in range can pick this up. There's no hacking involved, no grey area, the aircraft are announcing themselves to anyone who listens.

What makes it interesting to me isn't the data itself but what it implies. A flight from Istanbul to Frankfurt at 37,000 feet. A cargo plane out of Vienna at 2am. A small prop aircraft doing circuits around a regional airport fifty kilometres east. Each one is a story you can only see the outline of.

## The meditative part

I think what I actually like is having something to look at that changes slowly and predictably. The traces arc across the screen. New ones appear at the edges. Old ones fade when the aircraft moves out of range or the transponder goes quiet on descent.

It doesn't demand anything from you. You can watch it for thirty seconds or thirty minutes. It's the same kind of attention I give the Vrbas when I photograph it early in the morning, just presence, no agenda.

## The hardware

RTL-SDR V2 dongle, about 35 euros. A magnetic-mount antenna. An old USB cable. That's the whole setup. The software end is more involved but that's a separate post.

Charlie has no opinions about any of this, which I find reassuring.
