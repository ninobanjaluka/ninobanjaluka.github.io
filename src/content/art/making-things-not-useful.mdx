I have more than a few folders called `experiments` and they all have hundreds of files in it. Most of them are unfinished. Some are just screenshots I took because something looked interesting mid-process. A few are things I spent an entire evening on and then closed without saving the working file because I decided they didn't work.

I keep them all.

## Why experiments need to be failures sometimes

When I code something and it breaks, the goal is to fix it. The failure is a problem to be solved. When I make something visual that doesn't work, the failure is information about what I was actually trying to do, which I often don't know until I see something that misses it.

The four-hundred-file folder exists because I started letting experiments fail without trying to rescue them. A composition that's off isn't something to fix; it's something to understand and then abandon. The next thing I make will be slightly less wrong.

## What I'm actually working on

Lately I've been interested in the collision between sharp geometric shapes and organic noise. Hard edges with soft interiors. Grids interrupted by curves that shouldn't be there.

The current series uses a simple rule: start with a rectangle divided into a grid. Apply different Perlin noise offsets to each cell. Render each cell in a color derived from its position in the grid, shifted by the noise. The result looks like tiles that have been slightly displaced by some force that ran through them.

I don't know what the force is. That's fine.

## On showing work

I used to hesitate to show anything that wasn't finished or polished. I think this is common, and I think it's mostly a mistake.

The interesting thing about visual experiments isn't the output, it's the decision at each step. What happened when you changed the parameter. What you were trying for that you didn't reach. Showing an unresolved piece alongside why it's unresolved is more honest and more useful (to someone trying to learn to make things) than only ever showing the ones that landed.

Sometimes, ViewBug is where I put finished photography, for example, Shutterstock is where I sell them. Sometimes.At least that's the way it is, for now. Things change all the time. This blog is where I put everything else, including the unfinished things. And important things too.
